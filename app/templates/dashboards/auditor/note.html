Fieldwork Notes - Anixo Bank Ltd.Audit

Dashboard / Fieldwork Notes - Anixo Bank Ltd.Audit

Audit Period:

September 15, 2024 – October 5, 2024

Audit Team:

Lead Auditor, IT Security Auditor, Financial Auditor

Scope:
  • RBI Prudential IRAC Norms (Loan Classification and NPA Provisioning)
  • RBI Cybersecurity Guidelines (Data Protection, Access Controls, Incident Response, Third-Party Vendor Management, Financial Reporting)
Loan Classification and NPA Provisioning Controls (RBI IRAC Norms)
Data Protection and Cybersecurity Controls (RBI Cybersecurity Guidelines)
Access Control and Authorization Mechanisms
Incident Response and Recovery Procedures
Third-Party Vendor Risk Management
Financial Reporting and Reconciliations

Objective:
Ensure accurate classification of loans and provisioning for NPAs as per IRAC norms.
Summary of Findings:

Control Failures: 2 out of 50 loans were misclassified as performing loans, despite evidence of default exceeding 90 days. This resulted in incorrect financial reporting.

NPA Provisioning: 4 loans were identified with inadequate provisioning. The provisioning was below the RBI-recommended levels for loans classified as sub-standard.

Fieldwork Notes:
  • Discussions with the credit risk team revealed a lack of automated alerts for loan reclassification, leading to manual oversight.
  • Documentation supporting loan classifications was incomplete in 3 cases, raising doubts about data integrity in the bank’s credit risk management system.
  • The under-provisioning was due to errors in provisioning worksheets, possibly resulting from incorrect historical data on borrower performance.
Recommendations:
  • Strengthen automation in loan reclassification processes.
  • Conduct regular audits of NPA provisioning calculations to ensure compliance with regulatory requirements.
Objective:
Assess compliance with data protection and cybersecurity policies, focusing on encryption, access control, and incident response.
Summary of Findings:

Data Encryption: Encryption controls for data at rest and in transit were operating effectively, with compliance in 95% of cases.

Access Control Failures: 2 instances of unauthorized access to sensitive systems were identified, linked to poor role-based access control implementation.

Incident Response: The response time during a simulated security breach was delayed by over 20 minutes, indicating a gap in timely detection and escalation.

Fieldwork Notes:
  • Discussions with the IT security team showed inconsistency in user access reviews. Access logs indicated that roles had not been reviewed or updated for 6 months, creating a vulnerability in access management.
  • Testing of encryption systems confirmed that critical customer data was encrypted during transfers and while at rest on cloud servers.
  • During the incident response simulation, we observed delays in escalation and decision-making, largely due to a lack of clear ownership in the incident management plan.
Recommendations:
  • Regularly review and update access controls to ensure proper segregation of duties.
  • Redesign the incident response escalation process to reduce response times.
  • Conduct periodic access reviews and monitor privileged user activity.

Objective:

Evaluate the effectiveness of access control and authorization mechanisms to prevent unauthorized transactions and protect sensitive information.
Summary of Findings:

Access Control Gaps: Significant gaps were identified in the segregation of duties. For example, some users had both the initiation and approval rights for high-value transactions.

Authorization Mechanisms: High-value transactions were generally well-controlled, with appropriate approvals in 98% of cases.

Fieldwork Notes:
  • We observed outdated user roles and access privileges in the bank’s HR system. For instance, former employees still had access to critical systems, and cross-functional teams had overlapping access.
  • Verification of transaction logs showed that manual overrides were possible for certain transactions, bypassing normal approval flows.
Recommendations:
  • Implement stricter access governance controls.
  • Review and clean up user access lists regularly.
  • Enhance automation for authorization processes to eliminate manual overrides.
Objective:
Test the effectiveness of Anixo Bank’s incident response and disaster recovery plans to minimize downtime in the event of a cybersecurity breach.
Summary of Findings:

Incident Response: Delays in identifying and escalating security incidents were observed during the breach simulation. The bank's response time exceeded the acceptable thresholds.

Disaster Recovery: The recovery time objective (RTO) for critical systems was met for 90% of key systems. However, non-critical systems experienced delays in recovery.

Fieldwork Notes:
  • Interviews with the incident management team revealed gaps in role clarity during incident response, leading to slow decision-making.
  • A test of the bank’s backup systems confirmed that data restoration was effective, but delays occurred due to network congestion.
Recommendations:
  • Improve incident response times by assigning dedicated roles and responsibilities.
  • Review disaster recovery plans for non-core systems to ensure faster recovery.
Objective:
Ensure third-party vendors comply with the bank’s security policies and that vendor risks are properly managed.
Summary of Findings:

Vendor Risk Management: 20% of vendor contracts lacked adequate security clauses, increasing third-party risks.

Vendor Monitoring: Vendor performance monitoring was inconsistent. Risk assessments were not completed for 30% of vendors.

Fieldwork Notes:
  • Contract reviews highlighted security provisions missing from several vendor agreements. Interviews with the procurement team indicated this was due to a lack of standardized contract terms.
  • The risk management team had not conducted formal risk assessments for low-risk vendors, creating gaps in vendor oversight.
Recommendations:
  • Ensure all contracts include security clauses in line with RBI cybersecurity requirements.
  • Perform annual risk assessments for all vendors, regardless of risk profile.
Objective:
Review the accuracy of financial reporting and the timeliness of reconciliations.
Summary of Findings:

Delayed Reconciliations: Several key accounts had delayed reconciliations, with some accounts being reconciled over 30 days late.

Financial Reporting Accuracy: No material discrepancies were found in the financial statements.

Fieldwork Notes:
  • Discussions with the finance team revealed that staffing shortages led to delays in account reconciliations, particularly for lower-priority accounts.
  • The review of financial reports confirmed the accuracy of financial statement balances, with minor adjustments required for accrued liabilities.
Recommendations:
  • Address the staffing shortages to ensure timely reconciliations.
  • Continue to perform regular accuracy checks on financial reports.
Conclusion:
The fieldwork has revealed several control weaknesses in areas such as access control, incident response, vendor management, and financial reporting. The next phase will involve drafting remediation plans and discussing findings with the senior management at Anixo Bank.
