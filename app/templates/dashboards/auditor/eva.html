Executive Summary

This internal control evaluation is part of the ongoing audit of Anixo Bank Ltd. The evaluation encompasses both financial and operational controls, as defined by the RBI Prudential IRAC norms and Cybersecurity guidelines. Controls have been reviewed to assess their design, implementation, and operational effectiveness.

Areas of control reliance and control deficiencies are identified based on testing results, and this document provides recommendations for remediation where applicable.

Control Categories Evaluated

  1. Loan Classification and NPA Provisioning Controls (RBI IRAC Norms)
  2. Data Protection and Cybersecurity Controls (RBI Cybersecurity Guidelines)
  3. Access Control and Authorization Mechanisms
  4. Incident Response and Recovery Procedures
  5. Third-Party Vendor Risk Management
  6. Financial Reporting and Reconciliations

Areas of Control Reliance

Reliance can be placed on the following controls:

  • CO02: Provisioning of NPAs is effectively implemented and provides reasonable assurance of compliance with IRAC norms.
  • CO04: Regular reconciliation of loan account balances minimizes the risk of data discrepancies in loan records.
  • CO07: Encryption of customer data is effectively reducing the risk of unauthorized data access.
  • CO08: User activity logging is adequately designed and operational, providing evidence of proper control over critical systems.
  • CO09: Regular employee training has reduced risks from human errors in cybersecurity operations.

Control Deficiencies and Remediation Recommendations

Reliance can be placed on the following controls:

CO01: Loan Classification
Issue: Errors were identified in the classification of SME loans, resulting in misclassification of NPAs.
Recommendation: Implement automated checks and validations in the loan management system to prevent misclassification.
CO03: Access Control
Issue: Single-factor authentication is inadequate for critical systems.
Recommendation: Implement multifactor authentication for all critical systems and provide additional training for employees on authentication protocols.
CO05: Incident Response Procedures
Issue: No full-scale test of incident response procedures has been conducted, leading to potential gaps in readiness.
Recommendation: Conduct regular incident response simulations to assess the readiness of the bank to respond to cybersecurity threats.
CO06: Vendor Access Monitoring
Issue: Monitoring of external vendors is partially effective, with some access logs not adequately reviewed.
Recommendation: Automate log reviews and introduce real-time monitoring tools to assess vendor activities more effectively.

Conclusion

The internal control evaluation for Anixo Bank Ltd. has identified a mix of effective, partially effective, and ineffective controls. Significant gaps have been found in areas like loan classification and cybersecurity access controls, which require immediate remediation to ensure compliance with RBI Prudential IRAC norms and Cybersecurity guidelines.

Recommendations for strengthening controls and additional testing are provided to address the identified control deficiencies.
